# Business model and cash

## Translate proof into a business decision

| Question | Useful proof | Scope check |
|---|---|---|
| Who has a costly problem and can pay? | Recent actual buying or work behavior, observed friction, dated pricing and alternatives. | Identify buyer, user, approver, and budget owner distinctly. |
| How does the venture get the first and next purchase? | Channel access, conversion or purchase-intent evidence with its status, commercial terms, renewal or repeat mechanism. | Reach needs a defined population and a route to purchase. Stated intent is evidence about the respondent's statement. |
| What must be delivered at each unit sold? | Process, lead time, supplier quote, staffing and utilization, exception handling and constraints. | Align capacity with ramp-up, location, quality, season, and resource commitments. |
| What can be learned before the next spend? | Specific unknown, comparator, signal, cost, timeline and threshold for an operating decision. | Preserve any required prototype, permissions, and release terms. Avoid universal validation milestones. |

Where the rules do not prescribe a structure, choose relevant plain labels such as Ringkasan Eksekutif, Analisis Pasar, Penawaran, Model Bisnis, Strategi Pemasaran, Operasional, Proyeksi Keuangan, Tahap Implementasi, and Analisis Risiko. A buyer's actual decision should remain legible if the model is condensed to a few pages. Put detailed schedules and sensitivity exhibits in the appendix.

## Reconcile the model

- Build period volume from lead/reach, conversion, order frequency or retention, and feasible capacity. For recurring revenue, trace starting customers, additions, departures, and ending customers. For marketplaces, multiply covered gross transaction value by take rate where appropriate.
- Choose a complete sellable unit and state its net selling price. Deduct all direct costs needed to fulfill it, including direct service, transaction, return, and channel costs where applicable. Explain what the resulting contribution excludes. Break-even volume equals the relevant fixed costs divided by positive contribution per unit under constant price/cost and within usable capacity.
- Place collections and payments in the periods they occur. Tie opening cash, cash inflows, outflows, financing, and closing cash across periods. Identify the minimum cash position, the first funding need, and the milestone that funding enables. Tie inventory, receivables, and payables to operations.
- Choose conservative/base/upside assumptions or one clearly defined stress scenario when the decision hinges on uncertainty. Explain the driver, the plausible range and how a change affects hiring, capacity, pricing, or further spend. State when the plan depends on a pending commitment and assign responsibility for securing it.
- Check currency, forecast period, nominal versus real inputs, price basis, fees and taxes where relevant, and consistent treatment of one-time investment. Use NPV/IRR/payback only when decision-relevant, with cash-flow definitions and defensible discounting. Keep the logic inspectable in the workbook.

## Compact synthetic check

Example for testing a worksheet, not a market benchmark: 600 eligible orders meet a capacity limit of 500. At 500 fulfilled orders × Rp100,000 net revenue, revenue is Rp50 million. With Rp60,000 variable cost per order and Rp12 million fixed costs, operating contribution before other charges is Rp8 million. The break-even volume is 300 orders under the same assumptions. If only half the revenue is collected in the period and all Rp42 million of operating costs are paid, operating cash before financing or other items is negative Rp17 million. A plan that agrees with the profit number and disagrees with the cash number needs a collection or funding decision, not different arithmetic.
