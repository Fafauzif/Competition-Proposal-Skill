# Competition Brief and Scope

Read the brief, rubric, amendments, organizer answers and applicable template. Identify the edition and round. Check the request, earlier decisions, accepted text and any marked comments against those materials. Treat reference submissions as examples. Confirm the specific rules of the current round before carrying over requirements.

## Establish the scope

Record what matters for the task at hand:

- Audience and decision. Identify the judge, client, operator or investor and what the submission needs them to understand or decide.
- Required work. List the components the user asked for and the reasoning that has to survive inside each. A strategy section still needs its mechanism, rationale and implications after copyediting.
- Submission rules. Record required files, sections, languages, word/page rules and inclusions, permitted exhibits and appendices, formatting, acceptance criteria and deadline if relevant. Separate official requirements from house preferences and open questions.
- Evidence and commitments. Identify the available client data, external research, current draft, confirmed approvals and domains where missing access affects a conclusion. Collect only what the work needs.
- Boundaries for edits. Identify approved, marked and untouched areas. Ask before generalizing a comment outside its marked scope or changing a settled decision. Note adjacent dependencies such as citations or bookmarks that would be affected and ask before extending edits that change the audience-facing content.

Use [the intake examples](references/intake-and-scope.md) when the brief is hybrid, when page counts are ambiguous, or when a requested edit could affect existing work. A limited edit needs a limited check. A full intake can be kept in one private record rather than several documents.

## Choose the genre and mode

Determine the controlling judge question from the rubric and stage. Use Business Case Competition for a recommendation to a client, Business Plan Competition for a venture's commercial viability, and Innovation Essay for an evidence-led argument defending an innovation. Under a hybrid brief, route to the dominant task and add supporting skills as needed. Ask only if a material requirement or decision still has multiple plausible interpretations. Use Case Deck Design only when the user explicitly wants a BCC deck.

Match the current verb: brainstorm, assess, research, draft, revise, or produce. For an already authorized deliverable, use the plan to make it and finish within scope. Preserve earlier accepted choices unless new evidence or instructions require reopening them.

## Budget attention and space

Map the rubric and request to claims, main-text sections, supporting exhibits and likely space. Keep the minimum reasoning needed to understand the recommendation in the main submission. Put inspectable detail beside the exact claim in an appendix when permitted. With a tight allowance, reduce repetition and choose horizontally oriented exhibits before reducing essential argument or legibility. Follow the actual counting rule, including the treatment of references, appendices and covers. When a counting rule is ambiguous, record the interpretation and build conservatively if that keeps useful work moving.

Resolve reversible editorial or production choices yourself. Ask before selecting a material assumption about scope, conclusions, economic results or interpretation. Keep independent work moving while a consequential answer is pending. Give the question and its practical consequence in a short clarification, without asking for information already provided.

Before release, check the actual artifact against the current request and official brief. Verify every requested component and check for silent loss of analytical depth, rejected requirements accidentally reintroduced, and edits outside the agreed scope.
