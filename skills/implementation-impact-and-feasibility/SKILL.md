# Implementation, Impact, and Feasibility

Begin from the complete proposal as a service, business, strategy or technical system. Work backward from what must happen in use to what the responsible parties must deliver. Match depth to the decision and brief.

## Turn the operating model into a schedule

- Identify inputs, trigger, accountable role, authority, actions, handoff and output. Include a material exception, owner and recovery. Show proposed partners or dependencies as such. Where the organization lacks a capability, explain how the plan acquires it.
- Build phases around usable outputs. Each phase has a time window, owner, dependency, resource and observable completion condition. Tie staffing, equipment, legal access, supporting data and funding to the work that requires them.
- For a substantial implementation section, use a Gantt unless the required format dictates another useful schedule. Keep the main proposal Gantt wide and shallow with the phases and most important workstreams. Put detailed dependencies, owners and resource tables in the appendix when needed and permitted. Label estimated dates or durations as planning assumptions.
- Prioritize risks that can change adoption, budget, delivery, safety, regulation or interpretation. For each material risk, explain its trigger, consequence, prevention, response, owner and threshold for intervention. Tie the risk exhibit to the schedule or operating decision it affects. Use a heatmap only when the rating scale and decisions need one.

## Measure what the proposal changes

Define a short chain of inputs, work completed, end-user or operational outcome, and impact. Use only the measures that distinguish success from activity. Record numerator, denominator, unit, geography, period, baseline, collection method, owner, cadence and target basis as relevant. Explain the causal link or comparison the claim depends on. Reach is distinct from fulfillment and impact. Avoid extrapolating from a narrow population or a different operational setting without explaining the conditions for transfer.

Use a pilot, proof of concept, simulation or comparison when the current brief requires it or it resolves an uncertainty that changes what to do. Define the actual claim, eligible unit, intervention, comparison, measurement window, baseline, decision threshold, guardrails and next decision. Match the evidence to the claim. An internal demonstration can support a workflow step; a modeled outcome remains conditional. Place this work in its relevant implementation stage after the developed solution.

## Model feasibility and funding where needed

- **BCC:** Compare incremental contribution and cash against a credible counterfactual. Separate migration, cannibalization, discounts to existing demand and actual lift.
- **BPC:** Run a reachable funnel through demand, capacity and fulfillment to revenue. Model variable and fixed costs, startup costs, cash timing, funding tied to milestones, and downside risks. Include unit economics, cash runway or break-even where they affect viability.
- **Innovation essay:** Establish technical, operational, institutional and resource feasibility at a depth proportionate to the claims. Use costs, funding or economic comparisons when they materially support the idea.

State the model's unit, period, currency, scope and assumptions. Ask before making consequential new financial or interpretive assumptions. When the user authorizes modeling with assumptions, use defensible ranges and label them. If support is insufficient, show the break-even condition or decision threshold that would make the proposal workable. Use downside, base and upside scenarios where they change the decision, and distinguish model scenarios from statistical uncertainty.

Read [the engineering and economics worksheets](references/feasibility-worksheets.md) for operating phases, metrics, risks, finance and verification. Before release, reconcile the narrative, chart, workbook and appendices. Check dependencies, units, formulas, totals, accounting boundaries and actual readability.
