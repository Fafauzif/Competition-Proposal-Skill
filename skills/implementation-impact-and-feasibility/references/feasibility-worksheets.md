## Economics

1. **Demand and capacity.** Count reachable eligible opportunities, then apply relevant conditional conversion rates. Assign units and time bases at each step. De-duplicate under overlapping channels, account for repeat orders and cap fulfillment at the capacity that exists in the model. Separate TAM, SAM and obtainable volume by fit, geography, time, access and capacity.
2. **Revenue identity.** Units sold × net price, or paying accounts × revenue per account per period, or GMV × realized take rate. Use the identity that matches the business. Count price once.
3. **Contribution.** Subtract variable fulfillment costs, partner fees, customer acquisition where attributable, incentives and returns as appropriate. Preserve the treatment of any costs already included in the margin. For interventions, compare contribution with and without the proposal and deduct incremental fixed costs not included in that comparison.
4. **Cash and funding.** Include collection and payment timing, capex and working capital as relevant. Define tax treatment, operating boundary and period. Check the lowest cash position and the funding needed to reach the next delivery milestone. Profit, margin and usable cash answer different questions.
5. **Sensitivity.** Change drivers that can alter the recommendation such as adoption, price, margin, delay and capacity. Show a threshold and coherent scenarios where useful. Always label an assumption or projection. No universal payback or CLV:CAC ratio establishes viability.

For example, 100 additional fulfilled jobs at 80 currency units of contribution each bring 8,000 units. Discounts of 10 units on each of 300 existing purchases cost 3,000. With 4,000 in incremental setup cost, net incremental contribution is 1,000 under the scenario. The per-job margin already includes incentives on the 100 additional jobs. Illustrative arithmetic only.

## When validation changes a decision

Specify the result that could reverse or refine the plan. Choose a measurement method, time window and comparator matched to the claim. A field comparison can reveal operating friction. Establishing causal uplift requires an appropriate design and attention to selection, seasonality, spillover and concurrent changes. Put the appropriate success, revision or stop criterion before the decision to expand.
