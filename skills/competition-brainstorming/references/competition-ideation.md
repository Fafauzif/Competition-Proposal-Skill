# Competition Ideation Examples

## Develop an idea before changing it

The author proposes Lautara, a map for planning coastal monitoring at different depths. Develop the idea as an officer choosing a station, comparing modeled depth layers for the same date and location, recording a sampling question, and issuing a survey record that the crew can use and return. This exposes the important distinction between a model output for planning and a field observation for assessing a specific station. It also makes clear that a map is only one part of the complete solution.

A wide model cell might change the interpretation of the data, while leaving the planning workflow useful. Preserve the workflow and specify how the user checks the field reading. If the agency lacks authority or capacity to obtain the field profile and the sampling evidence is essential, compare achievable ways to deliver that step. Work around material constraints with explicit responsibilities rather than replacing the whole idea by default.

## Offer meaningfully different choices when asked

If a business case asks for new options to reduce repair abandonment, compare a clearer quote and appointment promise, an alternative fulfillment partner route, and a process change enabling same-visit completion. Each changes a different constraint. Renaming two quote screens would add little. Compare options under the same user segment, baseline, scope of work, and capacity assumptions wherever possible.

For an authorized recommendation, say which you'd choose and why. Where a business effect depends on an unknown conversion rate, explain the break-even point or outcome that would change the decision. Avoid numerical rankings with ungrounded weights.

## Use a benchmark precisely

1. Observe the exact behavior or operating bottleneck in the brief's scope.
2. Identify a real and attributable mechanism used elsewhere. Verify its current status where that status is material.
3. Explain what changes for the new operator, user, capacity, regulation, and economics. Keep any locally projected impact distinct from reported results elsewhere.

When the choices are known, name one and its practical consequence. “Does the agency issue the survey brief for its own crew or for a contractor? That determines who can edit it and who signs off.” Ask it only after checking what the brief already establishes.
