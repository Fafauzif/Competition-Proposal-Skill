# Selecting an exhibit

| Reader's question | Bundled family | Check before exporting |
|---|---|---|
| What explains this performance gap? | [Horizontal issue tree](../assets/charts/examples/issue_tree.json) | Causes follow from evidence. Each branch changes what the team investigates or does. Preserve the branching structure. |
| How does work move and where can it stall? | [Service and exception flow](../assets/charts/examples/service_flow.json) | Distinguish actions, transfers, decisions, and recovery. Trace an ordinary path and one failure path. |
| Who has authority over which work? | [Organization tree](../assets/charts/examples/stakeholder_structure.json) | Roles and authority relationships are real or explicitly proposed. |
| How do alternatives differ on two dimensions? | [Positioning map](../assets/charts/examples/positioning.json) | Explain both axes and the basis of each placement. Team placements are assessments. |
| Why would participants take part? | [Value proposition canvas](../assets/charts/examples/value_proposition.json) | Give each participant specific jobs and barriers and connect them to an action the offer enables. |
| How large is the opportunity and what can the team reach? | [TAM/SAM/SOM](../assets/charts/examples/market_layers.json) | Define segments, period, denominators, price, and penetration. The bundled nesting is schematic, with no area encoding. |
| Which work runs in which period? | [Gantt](../assets/charts/examples/gantt_year1.json) | Use explicit time units, phases, and dependencies. Treat a date or duration as planned unless verified. Connect to owners or deliverables in prose or a companion table. |
| Which risk scores call for action? | [Risk heatmap](../assets/charts/examples/risk_heatmap.json) | Explain the ordinal scale and use a companion register for trigger, owner, response, and residual exposure. Distinguish pre-control and residual scores. |
| How large is the difference across comparable groups? | [Horizontal comparison bars](../assets/charts/examples/comparison.json) | Compare like-for-like scope and units. Use a zero baseline so length conveys proportion. |
| How does a quantity change across time? | [Time-series lines](../assets/charts/examples/cash.json) | Keep time spacing correct. Label cash before or after financing as applicable. Distinguish actuals from projections where they meet. |
| Where do observations or draws concentrate? | [Distribution](../assets/charts/examples/npv_distribution.json) | Define the population, unit, and binning. Distinguish empirical observations and scenario draws. |
| Which inputs are associated with the result? | [Input–outcome association](../assets/charts/examples/drivers.json) | Describe the statistic and sample. Rank correlation does not establish causality. |

Use any form appropriate to the argument, including those outside the bundled renderer. A dumbbell plot can show paired changes. A dot plot can show estimates and intervals without filling the page with bars. Use a waterfall for a reconciled contribution bridge, a slope chart for two linked positions, small multiples for repeated comparisons, a choropleth for a comparable rate, and a schematic basemap for route or coverage choices. A swimlane can clarify authority during handoffs. Match all panels to a common scale when differences in size or position carry meaning. If another chart requires computation, preserve an editable input plus the method and reproduce the decisive values.

Keep chart captions concrete and close to the figure. Include material sources and assumptions without assistant or tool commentary. Use consistent keying and restrained color. When category identity matters, labels, markings, or line styles should carry it even in grayscale. Keep genuine exceptions, nonzero denominators, and unfavorable comparisons visible.
