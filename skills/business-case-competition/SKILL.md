# Business Case Competition

Identify the client, the decision it must make, the outcome that counts, and the resources or constraints the recommendation must respect. Use the current case brief and rubric. A general growth case can change into a profitability, capacity, or customer-fit case as the evidence develops. Ask briefly only if that interpretation or another major decision remains open.

## Build the case

1. **Frame the decision.** Define the business unit, segment, baseline, geography, period, and success measure. Separate the firm's objective from the customer's decision at the point of friction.
2. **Explain the performance gap.** Draw an issue tree if it helps locate the drivers. Give each consequential branch evidence, a rival explanation, and the decision that would change if that rival were right. Use the relevant operating data, interviews, survey, reviews, and market evidence already available. Different treatments of one dataset answer different questions and share the same source. Keep unsupported causes provisional.
3. **Use the user's idea as the starting point.** Develop it far enough to see how it works and where it could fail. When a substantive weakness surfaces, compare genuine alternatives on the same evidence, constraints, capacity, time, and economic basis. Show why the chosen option wins. Consult [Case analysis and exhibits](references/case-analysis-and-exhibits.md) for branch design, evidence and comparisons.
4. **Sell the complete recommendation.** Show a representative customer or operator using the finished system. Define the specific barrier, decision, handoff, accountable actor, and completed outcome. Explain how the proposed mechanism changes the client's performance. Where evidence supports distinct solutions, name each pillar and explain its contribution. One coherent intervention is sufficient where the brief and evidence support it.
5. **Translate the experience into value and delivery.** Count incremental outcome events against ordinary practice. Put the full outcome, contribution, dependency, and cost logic in view. Use scenarios or sensitivity when the decision hinges on conversion, capacity, timing, or cost. Work backward from the functioning recommendation to releases, owners, measures, and any required tests.
6. **Write the case for a decision-maker.** Lead with the supported recommendation in the executive summary. For analysis, use insight-led headings when a supported conclusion improves navigation. Introduce the named solution clearly, and reserve plain labels for conventional sections when they are easier to find. Explain the causal logic in the main submission and link each supporting sentence to the appropriate exhibit. Keep exact units, assumptions, and material adverse scenarios visible.

Use [Research, Evidence, and Citations](../research-evidence-and-citations/SKILL.md), [Proposal Strategy and Argument](../proposal-strategy-and-argument/SKILL.md), [Implementation, Impact, and Feasibility](../implementation-impact-and-feasibility/SKILL.md), and [Appendices and Document Production](../appendices-and-document-production/SKILL.md) according to the assignment. Use [Case Deck Design](../case-deck-design/SKILL.md) only when the user explicitly asks for a deck.

## Review the actual entry

Read the summary and headings as the client. Check whether the recommendation solves the stated problem, uses the evidence within its scope, closes the customer or operator journey, and distinguishes recorded events from additional outcomes. Reconcile the model, rollout, and exhibit destinations with the text. Use [Judge Review and Final Checks](../judge-review-and-final-checks/SKILL.md) for the complete submission.
